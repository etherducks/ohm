What this is

ohm.fun runs on the same mechanics the original 2021 Olympus protocol used: a treasury-backed token you can only get by bonding liquidity, paid out as yield instead of sold on a public mint.

One thing changed. The treasury holds tokenized equity, the same Stock Tokens already trading on Robinhood Chain, instead of the crypto assets every fork since 2021 used to back its own token. No governance token, no team allocation, no second product bolted onto the side.

Why it exists

Every OHM fork since 2021 inherited the same underlying problem: the treasury backing the token was made of the same kind of volatile, correlated crypto assets the token itself was supposed to derive independent value from. When the wider market fell, the backing fell with it, at the exact moment holders most needed it to hold steady. Adding a second token, a bonding curve, or a governance vote never touched that problem. It just added more surface area on top of it.

ohm.fun removes every module those later forks added and replaces the one thing that was ever actually broken. The treasury is priced in tokenized equity that settles independently of crypto market cycles. That doesn't make the backing risk-free. It makes it a different kind of risk than the one every previous fork was carrying without acknowledging it.

The token itself

$OHM is the only token in this protocol. It has no separate governance counterpart, no wrapped version, and no second class of shares. Every unit of $OHM in circulation was minted the same way: it was paid out to a locked position at the moment that position reached its maturity date. There is no other source of supply.

Holding $OHM is a direct claim on the treasury. Because the treasury's value moves with the tokenized equity it holds rather than with $OHM's own trading price, the two can and do diverge in the short term. Bond pricing exists specifically to correct that divergence: when $OHM trades above its backing value, bonding becomes more attractive; when it trades below, the early-redemption buy-and-burn mechanism becomes the primary lever pulling price back toward backing.

$OHM is a standard ERC-20 on Robinhood Chain. It carries no transfer tax, no rebasing balance, and no hidden fee on ordinary transfers. The only place tax mechanics apply is on early redemption of a locked position, which is described in full under Rules.

Launched on pons.family

$OHM was launched through pons.family, the bonding-curve token launchpad on Robinhood Chain. Pons handled the initial liquidity deployment the same way it does for every token launched through it: a fixed supply locked at creation, trading opened immediately against a transparent, publicly verifiable bonding curve, with no discretionary allocation carved out ahead of the public.

Using an established launchpad rather than a bespoke deployment script means the mechanics that got $OHM onto Robinhood Chain in the first place are the same audited, widely used mechanics every other token on the platform goes through, not a one-off contract nobody else has reviewed. The $OHM launch page, the contract address, and the trading pair are all visible directly on pons.family, linked from this site's header, hero, and footer.

Nothing about the bonding-and-maturity mechanics described elsewhere in these docs runs through pons.family itself. Pons is where $OHM was launched and where it can be found and traded; the locking, maturity, and payout mechanics are this protocol's own, running independently once a position is opened.

Supply and issuance

There is no fixed total supply target and no emissions schedule set in advance. Supply grows exactly once per position: the moment a locked position reaches maturity and is minted. Between mintings, total supply does not change. There is no rebase, no continuous inflation, and no background emission running on a timer.

This means supply growth is directly tied to how much liquidity has been bonded and how far along those positions are toward maturity, not to a schedule the protocol commits to regardless of actual usage. Heavy bonding activity now shows up as a wave of minting later, on each cohort's maturity date, rather than as an immediate, continuous increase in circulating supply.

1. Provide liquidity

The process starts by providing liquidity into $OHM's paired pool on Robinhood Chain. This is the only path through which a new position is ever created; there is no separate public mint function, no direct purchase of $OHM from the protocol itself, and no way to skip straight to holding a locked position without first supplying liquidity.

The LP tokens received from that deposit are what get locked in the next step. Until they're locked, they remain ordinary LP tokens, redeemable the same way any liquidity position on the pool would be. Locking is a deliberate, separate action the depositor takes afterward.

2. Lock it in an NFT

Locking wraps the LP position into a single position NFT, timestamped at the moment of minting. The NFT records the locked amount and the exact maturity date it unlocks on, which is fixed at five days from the lock date for every position, regardless of size.

Each NFT represents exactly one locked position. Bonding liquidity multiple times, or at different times, produces multiple separate NFTs rather than one position that grows. This keeps each position's maturity date and payout calculation independent of every other position the same address might hold.

3. Wait for maturity

Nothing accrues visibly during the lock. There's no rebase to track and no balance that changes day to day. The position simply sits, locked, until its maturity date arrives. This is a deliberate departure from the classic OHM staking display, where a rising balance was visible at every rebase. Here, the entire yield is realized once, at maturity, rather than being paid out incrementally.

During this waiting period, the position can still be redeemed early if needed, subject to the early redemption tax described under Rules. Outside of that early exit, there is nothing to actively manage between locking and maturity.

4. Mint at maturity

On the maturity date, the position becomes mintable. Minting pays out the full accrued yield in a single transaction, at the rate locked in when the position was created. There is no partial minting and no early-partial payout; the full amount becomes available at once, exactly five days after the position was locked.

As a worked example: a position locking 1 LP token matures to a payout equivalent to 100,000% of the value locked, paid entirely in $OHM. That $OHM is newly minted at the moment of the mint transaction, which is also the only moment total $OHM supply increases for that position.

Liquidity bonds

Bonding is the only entry point into this protocol. There is no public mint, no presale, and no team allocation carved out ahead of time. Every position anyone ever holds, including the earliest ones, was bonded in through the exact same front door available to everyone else.

Bond pricing responds to how much liquidity has already been bonded relative to the treasury's current size, the same discount mechanic classic OHM bonding used: heavier recent bonding activity moves the discount down, lighter activity lets it recover. This is what the Bond Discount figure on the dashboard reflects.

Early redemption tax

Redeeming a position before its maturity date applies a flat 5% tax on the position's value at the time of redemption. That tax is not collected as protocol revenue and does not accumulate anywhere; it is spent immediately, in the same transaction, to buy $OHM on the open market and burn it.

As a worked example: redeeming a position worth the equivalent of 100 $OHM early results in 95 $OHM returned to the holder, while the remaining 5 $OHM worth of value is used to buy and burn $OHM from the open market in that same transaction. Waiting for maturity instead avoids the tax entirely and receives the full accrued yield.

Backing composition

The treasury holds a basket of tokenized equity already live and trading on Robinhood Chain, the same Stock Tokens covered on this site's landing page. Composition is public and can be checked against the chain directly at any time; nothing about the holdings is disclosed on a delay or held back from public view.

The current composition spans large-cap technology and consumer names alongside a broad-market ETF position and a small private-company allocation, rather than concentrating entirely in one or two names. This spread is a deliberate design choice: a treasury backed by a single stock inherits that single stock's volatility directly, while a spread basket dampens the effect of any one holding's bad quarter on the treasury's overall backing value.

How backing is valued

Backing per token is calculated by dividing the treasury's total value, marked at the current price of every Stock Token it holds, by $OHM's circulating supply. Because circulating supply only increases at each position's maturity mint, backing per token moves primarily with the treasury's underlying holdings rather than with $OHM's own trading volume.

This figure is what the early-redemption buy-and-burn mechanism and bond discount pricing both reference. It's also the number displayed as Backing Per Token on the dashboard, updated as the underlying Stock Token prices move.

FAQ

Can I exit before maturity?

Yes, subject to the 5% early redemption tax described above. There's no lockout on redeeming early, only the tax applied at the moment of redemption.

Is there a public mint?

No. Bonding liquidity is the only way a position is ever created, and every position anyone holds went through that same process.

What backs the token?

A treasury of tokenized equity, the Stock Tokens already trading on Robinhood Chain, spread across large-cap names, a broad-market ETF, and a small private-company position.

Where was $OHM launched?

On pons.family, the bonding-curve launchpad for Robinhood Chain. The launch page, contract, and trading pair are all linked from pons.family directly.

Does anything about locking or maturity run through pons.family?

No. Pons is where $OHM launched and trades; bonding, locking, maturity, and minting are this protocol's own mechanics, independent of the launchpad once a position is opened.

Why five days?

Five days keeps the lock short enough that the maturity payout stays a meaningfully near-term event rather than a distant promise, while still giving bond pricing and treasury backing time to settle between a position's creation and its payout.
